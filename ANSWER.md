I used python through opencode to get the data off the Global Historical Climatology Network.
Opening and reading this data shows that there was an increase in temperature, most notable in the last 4 months of the year. December (1.5 degrees celsius) and September (1.3 degrees celsius) being the biggest increases just from 2001-2020 alone.
The graphs shown below also reveal that the mean temperature of the 20th century and the 21st century have experienced significant temperature increases.

Frost days have also been in the decline as well, from 159.5 days to 152.5.

<img width="1412" height="919" alt="fig5_precipitation" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ad21367d-bb9a-41c4-a40d-4418e87911a9" />
Now, you may be looking at the data and say precipitation has actually increased from 1086mm to 1206mm, but if you consider the mean temperatures and the mean daily minimum temperature, you can see that it has gotten warmer, and that means that this extra precipitation is more than likely rain instead of snow.
To answer this question, we need to actually look at the annual snowfall. The REPORT markdown file shows that according to the data we had compiled, the mean annual snowfall has actually increased from the time period of the last six decades of the 20th century (171mm) to the first two decades of the 21st century (177mm).

## The Answer
The answer to the question in hand is no, it actually snowed more in the first two decades of the 21st century just by 6 millimeters of annual snowfall. But it may not appear as so since the temperature has increased over time in the last 80 years while the annual precipitation had also increased, as per the graphs above this markdown.
